Health Report Card & Assessment Framework

What gets measured, how often, by whom, and how it turns into a per-student report with real suggestions a parent can act on — not just a spreadsheet of numbers.

Overview

How This Fits the Year

The 40-week curriculum carries three fixed screening checkpoints — baseline (Week 1), midterm (Week 20), year-end (Week 39–40) — for BMI and physical measures. Around those, a set of lighter-touch metrics run monthly or quarterly through student self-report and teacher observation. Everything rolls up into one Health Report Card per student, issued at three points in the year and walked through with parents at the matching Monthly Parent & Grandparent Virtual Meet.

Why Different Frequencies for Different Things

BMI and physical measures change slowly and need in-person tools, so 2–3 checks a year are enough and keep the camp burden manageable for a school. Habits (sleep, nutrition, hygiene) change week to week, so they're tracked monthly through the curriculum's own activities rather than a separate assessment — no extra classroom time needed. Mental wellness and menstrual health sit on a quarterly, confidentiality-first cadence deliberately — frequent enough to catch a problem early, infrequent enough to never feel like surveillance.

Data Flow

From Measurement to Report Card

2. PHC App or Paper Sheet
→
3. Rule-Based Suggestion Engine
→
4. Report Card Generated
→
5. Walked Through at Parent Meet

Camp results and monthly logs are entered against each student's PHC ID (paper form in Year 1 pilot schools; PHC app from Year 2). A simple rule-based lookup — not a diagnosis, just a plain-English nudge — turns each metric into one suggestion line (see below). The report card is generated at the same three points as the screening checkpoints and shared with parents at the matching Monthly Meet, with a 1:1 sign-up slot offered for anything flagged.

What This Means — Suggestions for Home
  • Book a dental follow-up within 4 weeks — the 2 flagged cavities are early-stage and simple to treat now.

  • Fitness trend is genuinely strong — no action needed, just keep up the weekly PHC circuit.

These Are Suggestions, Not Diagnoses

Every line on the card is a plain-English nudge generated from a simple rule, not a clinical judgement. Anything genuinely concerning (BMI extremes, dental issues, a mental-health flag) routes to the relevant expert for a real assessment before it ever reaches a parent as an action item.

Privacy

What Stays Confidential

Mental Wellness Data

Seen only by the school counsellor and the student. Never appears on the general report card unless the student and counsellor agree it should be shared with parents.

Menstrual Health Data

Collected only by a female facilitator, stored separately from the general record, shared with parents only at the student's or facilitator's discretion.

Haemoglobin / Medical Camp Data

Finger-prick or clinical checks require prior written parent consent per student — opt-out is always available with no penalty.

Data Storage

Paper forms in Year 1 pilot schools; from Year 2, the PHC app stores health data separately from general school records, access-restricted to the school nurse, counsellor, and the student's own parents.

Where This Plugs Into the Rest of PHC

Screening checkpoints live inside the 40-week curriculum (Week 1 / Week 20 / Week 39–40 in both category tracks). Results are walked through with families at the Monthly Parent & Grandparent Virtual Meet track — specifically the November and March sessions, which are built around the report card review.
